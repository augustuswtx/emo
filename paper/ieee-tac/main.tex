\title{From Degradation Response to Training Utility: Auditing
Fixed-Budget Auxiliary Learning for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis}

\begin{abstract}
Reliability scores may detect corrupted inputs, yet weighting auxiliary
supervision requires evidence that they identify trustworthy targets and
useful updates. We study
this gap in MFON-based multimodal sentiment analysis. An audit separates sample
granularity, response to controlled degradation, confounding, teacher--student agreement,
and fixed-budget allocation. We learn visual and acoustic degradation-response
scores from ordered feature corruptions and use them to redistribute per-sample
distillation and contrastive losses without changing each batch's mean
auxiliary budget. In a frozen three-seed CMU-MOSEI comparison, learned
allocation lowers mean absolute error by 0.0025 and raises correlation by
0.0007 relative to uniform allocation, while classification effects are mixed
and the repaired MFON baseline is not uniformly surpassed. The scores detect
in-family Gaussian corruption but transfer unevenly to unseen corruptions.
On validation data, visual scores are inversely associated with a negative-KL
teacher--student agreement proxy (mean Spearman correlation -0.3154); acoustic association is weak. In
one matched seed, inverse allocation attains slightly higher correlation than
learned allocation. Thus degradation detection does not establish that the
score provides a sound cross-sample training priority. The study contributes a
reproducible audit and equal-budget controls, while limiting its conclusions
to the tested backbone and benchmark settings.
\end{abstract}
