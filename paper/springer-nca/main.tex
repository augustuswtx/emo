\title[Audited Fixed-Budget Multimodal Learning]{Are Modality Quality
Scores Trustworthy? Audited, Fixed-Budget Auxiliary Learning for Multimodal
Sentiment Analysis}

% The review manuscript is double-blind. Submit author identities and contact
% information on the separate title page required by the journal.
\author*[1]{\fnm{Anonymous} \sur{Author(s)}}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}}

\abstract{Quality-aware multimodal learning assumes that a model can estimate
how trustworthy each modality is and use that estimate to regulate fusion or
optimization. This assumption is rarely audited: a quality score may collapse
to a batch-level scalar, track sequence length or feature energy, or be logged
without materially changing learning. Using MFON as the base model, we
introduce a five-part audit of sample granularity, degradation monotonicity,
confound sensitivity, actionability, and weight collapse. The audit exposes
concrete failures in an earlier prototype, including premature batch reduction
and a norm-based curriculum score that increases under stronger corruption. We
then learn visual and acoustic reliability from ordered clean--mild--strong
feature triplets and use the scores only to redistribute per-sample
distillation and contrastive losses. An allocation warmup preserves every
batch's mean auxiliary budget from the first epoch. Across three frozen
CMU-MOSI seeds, learned allocation improves the mean binary metrics, mean
absolute error, correlation, and test loss over an equal-budget constant
allocation; Acc-5 is tied and Acc-7 is lower by 0.0015. Reliability strongly
tracks synthetic degradation for vision (Spearman $-0.9629$, AUROC $0.9952$)
and audio (Spearman $-0.8199$, AUROC $0.9420$), although acoustic reliability
remains moderately correlated with sequence length. The evidence supports
audited training-time supervision allocation under a matched budget, but not
uniform gains, statistical significance, or inference-time robustness.}

\keywords{multimodal sentiment analysis, modality reliability,
quality-score audit, fixed-budget learning, auxiliary supervision}
