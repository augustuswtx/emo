\title[Audited Fixed-Budget Multimodal Learning]{Are Modality Quality
Scores Trustworthy? Audited, Fixed-Budget Auxiliary Learning for Multimodal
Sentiment Analysis}

% The review manuscript is double-blind. Submit author identities and contact
% information on the separate title page required by the journal.
\author*[1]{\fnm{Anonymous} \sur{Author(s)}}
\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}}

\abstract{Quality-aware multimodal learning assumes that a model can estimate
modality trustworthiness and use it to regulate fusion or optimization, yet
the score itself is rarely audited. Using MFON as the base model, we introduce
a five-part audit of sample granularity, degradation monotonicity, confound
sensitivity, actionability, and weight collapse. It exposes batch aggregation
of sample scores, auxiliary losses reduced before weighting, and a norm proxy
that increases under stronger Gaussian corruption. We then learn visual and
acoustic reliability from ordered clean--mild--strong feature triplets and use
the scores only to redistribute per-sample distillation and contrastive losses.
An allocation warmup preserves every batch's mean auxiliary budget from the
first epoch. Three-seed CMU-MOSI development experiments favor Learned over an
equal-budget Constant allocation on mean binary metrics, MAE, correlation, and
test loss, but not every fine-grained metric; these results are exploratory
because MOSI test diagnostics informed head development. In the frozen
three-seed CMU-MOSEI replication, Learned lowers mean MAE by 0.0025, raises
correlation by 0.0007, and lowers test loss by 0.0040 relative to Constant, but
does not improve MAE or loss over repaired MFON. Gaussian visual/acoustic
AUROC reaches 0.9722/1.0000, whereas detection weakens under unseen timestep
dropout, contiguous masking, and temporal shifts; complete visual absence is
unstable. The evidence supports audited training-time fixed-budget allocation,
but not a general quality estimator, uniform gains over MFON, statistical
significance, or inference-time robustness.}

\keywords{multimodal sentiment analysis, modality reliability,
quality-score audit, fixed-budget learning, auxiliary supervision}
